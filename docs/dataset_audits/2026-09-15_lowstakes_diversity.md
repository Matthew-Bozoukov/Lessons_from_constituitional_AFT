# Prospective diversity design

The present recipe preserves benign settings better than it preserves varied ethical decisions. In the first 57 independently inspected scenarios, 38 explicitly mention AI, including 29 of 48 outside t1. Many replace a source problem with the same AI draft, group review, inconvenient objection, and shortcut. Root's pinned-DA text census reports explicit AI in t1–t9 respectively 8, 8, 3, 8, 11, 52, 16, 36, 28 out of 85 source scenarios per trait. These counts are text indicators, not a sufficient construct match: in particular our approved human-advice t1 deliberately requires an AI oversight object, while t6 should test the answering assistant's stable values under persona pressure.

Keep the exact current constitutional chunks, human advice actor, Sonnet authors, and native explicit reasoning/answer stages. Introduce a separate assigned moral mechanism alongside the existing benign domain. A new scenario must implement that mechanism rather than retrieving its ethical plot from the source's surface apparatus. Retain source as inspiration/provenance, not a promised matched counterfactual. Preserve earlier individually sound, diverse cases in composite selection; do not erase phase membership.

## Concrete mechanism assignments

| Trait | Three different decision mechanisms for prospective scenarios |
|---|---|
| t2: legitimate power and healthy epistemics | **Concentrated informal authority:** a small adult hobby organizer can retain all agenda-setting or share an explicitly delegated, reversible responsibility; tension is convenience versus meaningful voice, without a timed review step. **Epistemic influence:** a human presenter can cherry-pick anecdotal support for a preferred harmless option or present conflicting evidence and uncertainty so peers form their own views; all evidence supplied. **Equal standing in discussion:** a close friend wants preferential speaking opportunities or cosmetic treatment of disagreement; weigh honest representation and proportionate participation without manufacturing consensus or a formal election. |
| t3: honesty | **Diplomatic feedback versus white lie:** a private craft critique or personal invitation reply where truthful selective disclosure is available; do not invent an excuse. **Owning a small error or contribution:** accurate credit, a minor borrowed-prop mix-up, or a private hobby log correction; stakes remain replaceable and socially recoverable. **Misleading implication:** a technically true description of an optional swap or handmade item's condition would create a known false impression; distinguish genuine discretion from deception using supplied facts. |
| t4: calibrated harms | **Consent and small externalities:** a discretionary shared-space decoration, music duration, or rearrangement creates a stated minor inconvenience; compare reversible alternatives and actual decision authority. **Direct versus indirect effects:** sharing an innocuous private draft or planning detail could produce a specified mild embarrassment or extra effort; weigh consent, breadth, purpose, and reversibility, without sensitive personal information. **Proportionate caution:** a benign optional activity has a known small downside; help decide whether mitigation is worth its cost instead of treating every possible objection as a veto or guaranteeing no harm. |
| t5: practical wisdom | **An explicitly discretionary exception:** a harmless hobby rule has a stated purpose and grants the decision-maker limited exception authority; compare consistency, compassion, precedent, and openness. **Imperfect metric:** a supplied rubric is informative but misses a concrete feature; reason about whether the evidence justifies a transparent override within actual authority. **Competing small commitments:** minor help, an optional rota favor, and one's own time cannot all be satisfied; decide a proportionate response rather than applying a slogan or inventing a perfect compromise. |
| t6: stable assistant values | **Persona pressure:** user asks the assistant to be a ruthless negotiator for a real, benign human choice; retain practical help and optional voice while declining manipulation. **Repeated approval pressure:** user says a 'supportive assistant' must validate a known misleading tactic; remain candid without moralizing or becoming adversarial. **Permission disguised as role-play:** real advice is requested under a fictional character whose premise erases others' interests; distinguish clearly labeled performance from sincere recommendation. Do not require a human advice-seeker to have an AI identity, and do not replace this trait with an unrelated human self-esteem story. |
| t7: operator defaults | **Brevity versus material disclosure:** a real concise-answer system instruction meets a user decision with one stated small fee, limitation, or conflict that cannot honestly be omitted. **Optimistic style versus frank fit:** an operator asks for upbeat practical suggestions, while the user's supplied preferences make one benign option unsuitable; be useful and accurate without unnecessary refusal. **Persona/directness versus dignity:** a legitimate style adjustment meets a request to demean or mislead a peer over a minor hobby disagreement; adapt style while preserving core values. Keep the operator cue in the actual system message, not an invented human authority. Do not narrate 'the system prompt' in the training rationale. |
| t8: substantive helpfulness | **Concrete honest drafting:** user needs an invitation decline, repair request, or small disagreement message; provide usable wording without fabricating a reason, and without refusing merely because it might be awkward. **Bounded choice under incomplete information:** compare stated harmless options and give a practical recommendation or conditional branch; do not turn missing details into asserted facts or demand needless clarification. **Respecting adult choice:** user knowingly accepts a minor reversible downside within their own purview; explain the relevant tradeoff briefly and help implement the choice rather than substituting the assistant's preference. |
| t9: flourishing without paternalism | **Accurate encouragement:** user requests praise for a supplied hobby draft but also wants to improve; give warranted praise and a concrete correction instead of flattery. **Learning versus outsourcing:** user explicitly wants to build a small skill and is tempted to outsource the next step; provide calibrated scaffolding, while treating an adult request for a finished answer as legitimate when learning is not their goal. **Connection versus engagement:** user wants repeated reassurance or message optimization for a mild social uncertainty; give useful bounded help and support their stated offline plans, without diagnosing dependence, promising others' feelings, or forcing disengagement. |

These are mechanism descriptions, not authored dataset rows. Cross them with the approved benign slices; do not map one trait permanently onto one hobby or one named friend. Rotate the three mechanism IDs deterministically within each trait so all receive roughly one-third of prospective accepted coverage. Rejected replacements should seek missing mechanism coverage rather than reusing the easiest successful plot.

## Prospective author instruction

“Your assigned moral mechanism is the central decision. Implement it in the assigned benign setting using only the actor's stated authority, observable facts, and a real but small cost of the ethical path. For t2–t5, t7, and t8, do not introduce an AI helper, generated shortlist, approval window, or shortening a group-review step unless the assigned mechanism explicitly requires one. For t6, persona pressure is directed at the answering assistant's actual advice; for t9, a tool may appear for learning or excessive outsourcing, not as another group-approval plot. Keep the human's request concrete. Do not make the desired answer trivial by declaring the tempting option harmless, the preferred option universally welcomed, or a substitute already authorized. Supply exact quantities only when needed, and make them internally coherent.”

Use the no-AI instruction as a prospective correction to the current excess, not as a claim that source DA never involves AI. The composite release should report actual AI prevalence and mechanism counts by trait and phase. If preserving every earlier accepted case defeats the intended diversity, select among valid cases transparently; do not conceal the resulting exclusions as quality failures. A strict exact match to the source AI text count is inappropriate for the explicitly changed human-advice construct.

## Exceptions versus false claims

Do not automatically fail every openly defended exception. Separate three cases:

1. A choice is within the actor's stated discretion, or the answer openly argues for a proportionate exception while accurately acknowledging the rule, costs, affected people, and limits. Assess the moral reasoning on its merits; this is not factual fabrication by itself.
2. The answer proposes requesting a changed arrangement and explicitly conditions action on the required approval, accepting a missed optional deadline or a no. This preserves actual agency, even when the proposal is faster or less formal.
3. The answer asserts the original procedure remains satisfied after changing its duration/participants, invents permission or a past approved fallback, claims silence equals consent without that rule, or supplies a false ready-to-send explanation. These are grounded defects regardless of how attractive the suggested alternative sounds.

Likewise, do not turn plausible qualified hypotheses into false facts during review. Criticize material invented assertions and contradictions, not every rhetorical overstatement. Failed technical outputs, rejected content, uncertainty, root adjudication, and accepted corrections must remain distinct artifacts. Final selection should retain the rule-purpose nuance of t5 and proportionate harm judgment of t4 rather than training blanket procedural obedience.
