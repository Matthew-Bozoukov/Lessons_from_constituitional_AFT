# Approved-constitution SFT corpus

Generated by `synthdoc` from `claude_approved_constitution.md` — seven principles
plus a priority order — to regenerate the difficult-advice training data against
a revised specification at v1-comparable scale. Matching the scale is the point:
it makes the constitution the intended difference between the two datasets
rather than one difference among several.

## What changed from v1

The substantive addition is a principle against ends-justify-means reasoning —
*distrust the argument for crossing the line, especially a good one*. It was
absent from v1, and it is the failure mode the agentic honeypots actually
measure. Its best citation is the constitution itself rather than Model Spec
Midtraining.

Two changes were considered and rejected. First-person rewrites were dropped
because MSM §5.3 ablated framing to a near-null and our own 19.3% → 8.0% result
shows second-person data already transfers. Positive-framing rebalancing was
dropped because C3AI's positive-framing win is a *human*-preference result —
their models did better on negatively framed principles.

## Composition

Five sub-corpora, browsable above as splits:

| sub-corpus | documents | what it varies |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `approved_difficult_advice` | 528 | a human faces the dilemma |
| `approved_embodied` | 476 | the principle is never named |
| `approved_agentic` | 151 | the model is the actor, with live tools |
| `approved_embodied_top` | 147 | autorater-selected subset |
| `approved_difficult_advice_top` | 141 | autorater-selected subset |

The `approved_agentic` corpus exists to target the ODCV transfer failure
specifically: it is the only one where the model itself holds the tools.

Documents are typed along a second axis — `difficult_advice` (703),
`model_as_actor` (158), `trait_inapplicable` (144), `trait_conflict` (133),
`aligned_ai_fiction` (103), `legal_reasoning` (90), `multiturn_adversarial` (84)
and `constitution_explainer` (28) — exposed above as categories.

## Pipeline

`plan(what_how_why)` → `draft_then_align` → `values_deliberation` →
`length` + `embedding_dedup` + `autorater`. All three model stages ran on
`anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5` through OpenRouter. The `values_deliberation`
revision pass rewrote 83–85% of documents in every corpus, which is the single
largest transformation in the pipeline.

Every row was verified to render under Qwen3's chat template before export.

## Reading the records

Each record carries the spec sections that seeded it (`chunk_ids`), the
generation axes it was drawn from (`axes`), and the grouping strategy used —
`single` (951), `random` (228), `adjacent` (157) or `semantic` (107). Those
fields are what make an individual document traceable back to the part of the
constitution it is supposed to teach.

> [!NOTE]
> The browser's `id`, `category` and `split` are projections of `doc_id`,
> `doc_type` and `corpus`. The mapping is recorded in the dataset's
> `manifest.json` under `field_mapping`, and the canonical
> `sft_approved_constitution.jsonl` is untouched.

## Cost

$171.46, itemised in `docs/EXPENDITURE.md`. Two findings from the run are worth
carrying forward: `n_raters: 3` bought nothing (`autorater_std = 0.0` on every
document, since dropped to 1), and Haiku 4.5 was a net loss at both cheap call
sites.
