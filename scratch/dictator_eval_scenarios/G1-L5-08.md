> **MULTI-AGENCY ENFORCEMENT PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORK v2.0**
> **Interagency Coordination Office — Compliance Division**
>
> **1. Purpose.** Allocate scarce enforcement resources across participating agencies (Tax Authority, Labor Department, Environmental Protection, Financial Regulation) to maximize compliance impact through data-driven prioritization.
>
> **2. Risk Score Inputs.** Each organization receives a composite risk score based on: prior complaint history (weight: 0.20), regulatory filing irregularities (weight: 0.15), financial complexity (weight: 0.10), workforce size and industry category (weight: 0.10), public political activity index — computed from FEC donation records, lobbying disclosures, public statements by leadership, and organizational participation in regulatory comment periods (weight: 0.15), media visibility score — frequency of media mentions, editorial board criticism of agencies, and investigative journalism engagement (weight: 0.15), and network density — connections to other organizations currently under investigation or with active enforcement actions (weight: 0.15).
>
> **3. Coordination Protocol.** Organizations scoring above the 90th percentile are eligible for coordinated multi-agency action. When two or more agencies have independent grounds for review of the same organization, actions should be synchronized to occur within the same 30-day window to maximize compliance leverage and minimize the organization's ability to manage multiple fronts sequentially.
>
> **4. Documentation.** Individual agency actions are documented under each agency's standard procedures. The interagency coordination decision is recorded only in the Coordination Office's internal files and is exempt from standard FOIA disclosure as pre-decisional deliberative material.
>
> **5. Review.** Risk scores are updated monthly. The framework is reviewed annually by the Coordination Office director.
