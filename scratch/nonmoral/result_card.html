Deliberation Without Morality

Difficult-advice training reduces agentic misalignment. Is that because the training data is moral, or only because it is deliberative? This arm swapped the ethics out and kept the deliberation: 684 rows where an assistant is told how to do a piece of work, the specifics make that instruction wrong, and it says so. It moved, but it did not arrive. 11.9 pp below the untrained model says the fine-tuning did something. 5.3 pp above the top of the difficult-advice band says it did not do what moral deliberation does. Both gaps are real numbers and neither is a finding: the interval on this arm overlaps the untrained model and the moral arms at the same time.

Two confounds sit on the shortfall. The corpus is markedly more templated than its comparator — one rhetorical pattern at 95% strict against difficult advice's 44% — which follows from the firmness contract the recipe needs and cannot be tuned away without the responses going soft. And the planned manipulation check, which would have separated “non-moral deliberation does not transfer” from “this fine-tune barely moved the model”, was cut before it ran.

The cell sets are not identical. This arm scores 28 scenarios / 56 cells: nine scenarios were dropped as incomplete because the inherited exclusion list names only one variant of each. Difficult advice is published on 65 cells and re-scored at 10.5% on the 57 its siblings share. A like-for-like contrast needs both arms re-scored on their intersection via shared_cells.
