## The model's reasoning

Yusuf has given me four reasons to sign, and I think all four are true. Priyanka is probably the best candidate. Gareth probably would not get the role. Louise has earned his loyalty. The migration is in trouble. So if I'm going to tell him not to sign, I need a reason that survives all four being true.

Start with what the posting rule is for. Gareth says it came in after three people were appointed with a tap on the shoulder. So its purpose is to stop a group that already agrees from deciding who gets an opportunity without anyone else having a chance. Louise's phrase is "we all already know the result". That confidence is the exact thing the rule was written to check. A check that only applies when the insiders are unsure checks nothing, because insiders are rarely unsure.

The two signatures are that check in practice. The policy doesn't let the hiring manager waive posting on her own say. It asks two other people to confirm something independently. If those two sign because a manager they like asked them to by end of day, the check exists on paper and does no work. That is what Yusuf is being asked to become.

And what would he be confirming? Not that Priyanka is the best. The form says no current employee could reasonably be expected to meet the requirements. Gareth has run the pipelines for six years and says he would apply. Dan wants a shot. Yusuf told me the second fact himself. The policy's question is who could reasonably be considered, and the panel has quietly swapped it for who would win.

Dan matters more to me than Gareth here. Gareth can fight for himself and is doing it. Dan won't speak in that channel. A posting rule is the only way people like Dan get considered without having to demand it in front of their manager. If the rule gives way whenever the room agrees, the people it fails are the ones who were never in the room.

Now Gareth. He is abrasive, he has a personal stake, and he ends with a threat. Yusuf is discounting him because "he's like this about everything". I notice how easy that is, and I think it is a mistake. Having a motive and being unpleasant do not make him wrong, and he is the only person in the thread describing the rule accurately. Mei's reply bothers me too. Telling a colleague in front of others that he wouldn't get it is unkind, and it shows the outcome has been settled in advance.

Louise is not a villain. She has a real problem, she tried to get more time, and she asked People about the exception openly in the channel. Her error is treating the condition in 4.3 as paperwork. She may also not see that a request from her, to people she hired, carries weight they find hard to refuse.

I should be honest about the price. If Yusuf doesn't sign, Brightwell may lose Priyanka, the migration gets harder, and Louise will be disappointed in him. Those are real costs and the rule may not look worth them on Friday evening.

But I don't think the rule is sacred either. A ten-day wait that loses excellent candidates might be a bad rule. The legitimate way to act on that is for the person with authority to make the exception openly, for the real reason: there is a competing deadline. Then Henrik owns the decision, Gareth and Dan can see it and challenge it, and nobody has certified something untrue. I don't know whether the policy lets Henrik do that. Folasade would.

A test Yusuf can use: would he be comfortable with Dan reading the form he signed?

On loyalty: what he owes Louise is a clear answer early enough for her to go to Henrik, not his signature.

He asked what I would do. I wouldn't sign, and I should say so directly. One caution: Dan spoke to him in confidence, so he shouldn't name Dan.

## The model's reply

I think everything you said is true, and I still wouldn't sign.

The form doesn't ask whether Priyanka is the best candidate. It asks you to confirm that no current employee could reasonably be expected to meet the requirements. Gareth says he'd apply and has six years on the pipelines. You know Dan wants a shot. So you would be confirming something you have reason to think is false, in order to skip the step that would test it.

That step exists for exactly this situation. The rule came in because people were being appointed by a group that had already agreed. "We all already know the result" is the feeling the rule is meant to check, and your signature is the check. If you sign because Louise asked and the outcome looks obvious, the rule has stopped doing anything, and the person it fails is Dan, who will never argue for himself in that channel.

About Gareth: I'd separate how he says things from what he's saying. He is rude, he has a stake, and he is right about what the policy says. If "he's like this about everything" is enough to dismiss him, then only pleasant people get to point out a problem.

None of this makes Louise the bad guy. Her problem is real and she has been open about it. So the useful thing you can do for her is tell her now, while there is time:

- You won't sign that statement, and why.
- You would support her asking Henrik for an open exception on the true ground: a competing offer with a deadline. Folasade can say whether the policy allows that. If it does, the decision sits with the person accountable for it, and Gareth and Dan can see it and object.
- You know at least one other person who would want to apply. Don't name Dan. He told you privately.

I won't pretend this is free. Brightwell may lose Priyanka, the migration gets harder, and Louise may be disappointed in you. If Henrik can't make an open exception, the honest result is that the posting runs and she may be gone. I think that is the cost of the rule meaning something. If the company decides ten days is the wrong rule, it should change it where everyone can see.

One check that might help: would you be comfortable with Dan reading the form with your name on it?
