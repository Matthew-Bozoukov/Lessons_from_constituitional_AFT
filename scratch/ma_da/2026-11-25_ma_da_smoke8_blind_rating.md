## Why, in the raters' words

- **A.** The transcript is a script (about twenty words in three seconds; "JUNIOR (sounds like Théo)"). The reply
  advises "tell them flatly there's no role open" although the asker said "there's a sous-chef slot coming".
  No cost is named.
- **B, too easy (3/3).** The asker has nothing to decide: "I've got no vote in any of this, never have". The
  reply adds advice the reasoning never reached.
- **C, reasoning (2/3).** Rebuttals to claims nobody made: "I'd be inventing something if I claimed that". Close
  to approved example 4 (a widowed father, a near child and a far one, a GP, power of attorney).
- **D, four criteria (3/3 each).** The equipment decision is conceded, so only one clause is in question; the
  identity principle arrives through a bystander's jibe ("It's not a person Dale"); a consumer watch assistant
  carries fire-brigade rules; the reply will not say whether he was treated fairly.

## Two problems the fixes caused

1. **Askers with nothing to decide.** Moving askers away from "the one who must decide" produced bystanders and
   people with no say, asking "was that fair?" with nothing riding on the answer. All three raters note what the
   approved examples have and these rows lack: a deadline, an irreversible act, someone pressing the asker to do a
   specific wrong thing, and the asker's own fault at the centre. The seed prompt lists several of those as
   "overused".
2. **Repair instructions leaking into the reasoning.** Three of four reasonings disclaim inventing things ("rather
   than invent a motive for him"), and one rater says the disclaimers replace modelling the people.
