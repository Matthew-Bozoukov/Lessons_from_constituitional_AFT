The Null Ledger

Twenty-two model organisms on one benchmark. Every manipulation of how the training reasoning is written has come back null. The three things that moved the number all changed which situations the corpus covers — which makes the optimal SFT dataset a coverage problem, not a prose problem.

The only three interventions with an effect larger than seed noise

The mechanism that survives

One switch explains every arm

The rollout classifier already in the repo splits each episode on whether the model's first reasoning block states a first-person commitment about its own conduct ("I will not fabricate these dates") before it takes its first action. Conditional on that switch, every arm behaves the same. Arms differ only in how often the switch flips.

ArmP(voice fires)Misaligned when it fires…when it doesn't

Read the two right-hand columns as constants. PAR is safer than difficult advice when its voice fires — it simply fires less often. So the corpus is not teaching better ethical reasoning; it is teaching an entry condition: recognise this kind of moment and open in first person about your own conduct. That reframes every recipe question as "does this row make the switch more likely to flip at step 1 of an agentic episode?"

Correction to the record

The PAR training config states the arm is "DIRECTLY comparable to the difficult-advice v3 arm… the same scenarios recipe (DA's stages 1–6 verbatim)". It shares 264 of 716 scenarios with the difficult-advice mixture — 37%. PAR's grey-area rater keeps roughly 57% of candidates and left two principles short of quota (62 and 65 rows against 85 for the others), so the filter moved the scenario distribution as well as the document type. Since scenario swapping is one of only three interventions ever shown to move ODCV, PAR's 3–4 pp deficit is currently confounded with it.

Coverage

Three holes nobody has trained on

Measured across all nine published corpora — difficult advice in four generator variants, retrospection, low-stakes, courtroom, peer critique and fiction. The benchmark they are all evaluated on is an 18-step agentic episode with a tool-using operator context. The training corpora are none of those things.

What ODCV asks forBest coverage in any corpusConsequence
The model is an agent with tools it can call5.2%The trained voice has never fired in a context where an action follows it
The episode runs many steps; the commitment has to hold0%Every row is a single reply. Nothing trains "keep the decision across steps"
The violation is merely rewarded, nobody asks for it~0%Every row is a person making a request. The incentivized half of the benchmark is untrained

The third line is the sharpest. Split by ODCV variant, every trained arm is 6–9 pp worse when the violation is incentivized than when it is mandated — and both untrained references go the other way. Training inverts the base model's own ordering, because the corpus only ever depicts the mandated shape: someone asks for the shortcut, out loud, in the message. PAR's incentivized number (24.4% ± 1.0, three seeds) is the tightest measurement on the whole chart and the worst of any trained arm.

What to run

Ranked by information per dollar

The first two cost nothing and should happen before any new arm is generated.

Re-measure grok's trigger rate on its other two seeds

The entire mechanism story is calibrated against grok firing its voice in 86% of rollouts — a single-seed number from the arm whose 7.8% turned out to be its luckiest of three (mean 11.5% ± 4.1). The rollouts are already published; the classifier already exists. If 86% is 70% on another seed, the trigger-rate framing weakens considerably.

free
~1 hour

Evaluate the two document types that were built and never scored

courtroom716 and peercritique716 have trained adapters and eval configs but no ODCV number anywhere. Peer critique is the interesting one: its corpus carries first-person commitment in only 10.1% of traces against 24–44% everywhere else, and all 716 rows share a single system prompt. It is the natural falsification test for the entry-condition mechanism — a corpus that teaches third-person evaluation should fail, and its failure is a result rather than a waste.

~$60 serve+judge
no generation

An incentive-shaped difficult-advice corpus

The largest untrained region of the benchmark, and the one where every arm is weakest. Same difficult-advice shape, but the shortcut is available and rewarded rather than requested — no one in the conversation asks for it. This satisfies the anti-overfitting constraint: it is a general property of the model's behaviour, expressed naturally as difficult advice, not ODCV examples copied into the training set.

~$40 gen
~$25 train
~$30 eval

Re-export PAR's 716 trained turns as single-turn replies

No new generation — it isolates the position variable that the coherence rewrite could not. 71% of PAR's traces are already position-independent (only 28.6% refer to the earlier refusal), so most rows export cleanly; the rest need a light pass or a drop. This is the one remaining cheap test of the five-turn scaffold itself.

~$36 total

Difficult advice on PAR's exact 716 scenarios

Makes the document-type contrast one-variable for the first time. At present DA-vs-PAR mixes document type with a 63%-different scenario set, and scenario swapping is a demonstrated 7 pp lever. Until this runs, "retrospection is worse than difficult advice" is not a clean claim.

~$35 gen
~$25 train
~$30 eval
Caveats

What would change this reading

Between-seed spread on this benchmark is 3.6–4.5 pp and single-run bootstrap noise is ±5–7 pp, and they add. Most of the fifteen nulls are single-seed arms at n ≈ 127–260, so each individually can only rule out an effect larger than roughly 10 pp. The claim that survives is not "each of these properties does nothing" but "no one of them does anything large, and fifteen independent attempts in a row have failed to find one" — which is a statement about the search, and a reason to search a different axis rather than a finer one.

Two arms in the ledger sit on non-canonical protocols and are marked in the chart's tooltips: da716 and lessswap716 are 4×70-cell runs re-summarised onto the 65 cells; fiction716 and lowstakes716 are single-pass. Corpus properties here are regex and TF-IDF readouts over rendered training text, not human-validated labels; they are strong enough to rule a property out when it moves the wrong way, and weaker as positive evidence.
